Current campaigns

Future Booth

In the fall, the MA Senate and House will negotiate the final version of the Mass Wins Act, a large economic development bill. One of the major provisions they will decide is whether the Transparency in Frontier Artificial Intelligence Act forms part of the final law. This chapter of the package would require large AI developers to:

  1. Create enforceable safety plans
  2. Submit risk reports
  3. Report critical safety incidents
  4. Run third-party audits
  5. Give their workers whistleblower protections

These crucial policy standards will directly impact the large AI corporations whose irresponsible development practices, due to a lack of regulation, are already causing harm. These regulations will help keep Bostonians and Massachusettsans safe.

Creatives for Collective Action partnered with the Massachusetts AI Action Network (MAIAN) to create an art justice and AI safety advocacy tabling campaign named Future Booth. Five CCA artists designed and built the portable booth. The booth was brought to public places around Boston to reach the people directly impacted by the bill. MAIAN tabled alongside the booth to inform community members about the bill, why AI safety matters, and encourage people to use the phone booth to call their representatives.

Inside the booth, visitors found a script of what to say to their representatives when they called. Afterward, callers wrote their reasons for calling directly on the booth windows, becoming part of the art project. The goal was to make advocacy immediate, public, creative, and fun rather than simply asking people to promise they would call or engage in some other way later. This project generated over 2,000 conversations and hundreds of calls.

What Is an Art Build?

An art build is a gathering where artists, organizers, and community members create visual materials together for a campaign, event, action, fundraiser, or community need.

Art builds can produce banners, signs, posters, zines, props, installations, digital graphics, or other public-facing creative work. Just as importantly, they create space for relationship-building, skill-sharing, political education, care, and imagination.

Accessibility & Care

Our gatherings are participatory, welcoming, and beginner-friendly. You do not need formal art training to attend. We want our events to be as welcoming and accessible as possible. As we plan each gathering, we will share information about location, transit access, food, masking/COVID expectations, materials, physical accessibility, and any participation needs.
